Materials & Maintenance

The Woods We Work With, and How to Look After Them

Solid wood changes a little over time — that's part of its appeal. Here's what to expect from each species we use most, and how a few minutes of care keeps a piece looking right for decades.

Species We Use

Four Woods, Four Personalities

Teak

Golden-amber tone with a pronounced, elegant grain. Naturally rich in protective oils, making it exceptionally stable and highly resistant to moisture.

Durability

Luxury living room furniture, outdoor decks, and high-traffic surfaces.

Nedun

Deep, rich chocolate-brown with distinctive dark streaks. An exceptionally dense timber, celebrated for its extreme durability and premium, heavy-duty feel.

Durability

Heavy-duty inline sliding doors, luxury dining tables, and structural statement pieces.

Mahogany

Warm reddish brown luster with a fine, uniform grain. Highly stable, warp-resistant, and darkens gracefully into a rich, deep hue over time.

Durability

Elegant bedroom suites, refined interior cabinetry, and detailed carvings.

Jackwood

Vibrant golden-yellow tone that matures beautifully into a warm, rich brown. Highly resistant to pests and renowned for its incredible longevity.

Durability

Traditional household furniture, custom kitchen cabinets, and structural frameworks.

Care Guide

Keeping It Looking Right

Wipe spills with a soft, slightly damp cloth and dry immediately. Avoid leaving wet cups or plant pots directly on an unprotected surface — standing water is the main cause of white ring marks on any finish.

Once a month, dust with a dry microfibre cloth, then apply a thin coat of food-safe mineral oil or beeswax finish to oiled pieces (skip this step for lacquered or painted furniture). Buff gently with a clean cloth until the surface feels smooth, not tacky.

Solid wood expands and contracts slightly with humidity changes across seasons. Keep pieces a short distance from direct heating vents or radiators, and consider a room humidifier in very dry, air-conditioned spaces to reduce hairline surface checking.

Light surface scratches on oiled furniture can often be buffed out with a little extra oil worked in with fine steel wool, following the direction of the grain. Deeper scratches or dents are best brought back to us — it's covered under the two-year guarantee on structural work, and we keep matching finish stock for touch-ups after that.

Most woods shift colour slightly with UV exposure — walnut lightens a touch, Mahoginy and Teak deepen and warm up. This is normal ageing, not damage. Rotating rugs, cushions, or table runners occasionally helps the colour shift evenly across a surface.
